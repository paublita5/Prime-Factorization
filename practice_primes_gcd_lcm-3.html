Part B: GCD & LCM via Prime Decomposition
For each pair, find gcd and lcm as plain numbers.
6. $(24, 36)$ — find $\gcd$ and $\operatorname{lcm}$.
$24 = 2^3\cdot3$, $36 = 2^2\cdot3^2$
$\gcd = 2^2\cdot3 = 12$   $\operatorname{lcm} = 2^3\cdot3^2 = 72$
Common divisors: $1,2,3,4,6,12$
7. $(50, 84)$ — find $\gcd$ and $\operatorname{lcm}$.
$50 = 2\cdot5^2$, $84 = 2^2\cdot3\cdot7$
$\gcd = 2$   $\operatorname{lcm} = 2\cdot5^2\cdot2\cdot3\cdot7 = 2100$
Common divisors: $1,2$
8. $(252, 270)$ — find $\gcd$ and $\operatorname{lcm}$.
$252 = 2^2\cdot3^2\cdot7$, $270 = 2\cdot3^3\cdot5$

Common divisors: $1,2,3,6,9,18$

Part C: Euclidean Algorithm
Use the Euclidean Algorithm (NOT prime factorization) to find each GCD. Enter just the final number.
11. $\gcd(252, 198)$ — show every division step.
$252=198\cdot1+54$
$198=54\cdot3+36$
$54=36\cdot1+18$
$36=18\cdot2+0$
$\gcd = 18$
12. $\gcd(840, 231)$ — show every division step.
$840=231\cdot3+147$
$231=147\cdot1+84$
$147=84\cdot1+63$
$84=63\cdot1+21$
$63=21\cdot3+0$
$\gcd = 21$
13. $\gcd(1734, 1215)$ — show every division step.
$1734=1215\cdot1+519$
$1215=519\cdot2+177$
$519=177\cdot2+165$
$177=165\cdot1+12$
$165=12\cdot13+9$
$12=9\cdot1+3$
$9=3\cdot3+0$

14. $\gcd(987, 610)$ — show every division step.
These are consecutive Fibonacci numbers — the worst case for the Euclidean Algorithm (every quotient is 1):
$987=610\cdot1+377$, $610=377\cdot1+233$, $377=233\cdot1+144$, $233=144\cdot1+89$, $144=89\cdot1+55$,
$89=55\cdot1+34$, $55=34\cdot1+21$, $34=21\cdot1+13$, $21=13\cdot1+8$, $13=8\cdot1+5$,

Part D: Using the GCD–LCM Identity
Recall: $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b) = \dfrac{a\cdot b}{\gcd(a,b)}$
15. Given $\gcd(861, 1127) = 7$, find $\operatorname{lcm}(861, 1127)$ without factoring.
$\operatorname{lcm} = \dfrac{861\cdot1127}{7} = 138{,}621$
16. Given $\gcd(1428, 3315) = 51$, find $\operatorname{lcm}(1428, 3315)$.
$\operatorname{lcm} = \dfrac{1428\cdot3315}{51} = 92{,}820$
17. Use the Euclidean Algorithm to find $\gcd(714, 1326)$, then use the identity to find $\operatorname{lcm}(714, 1326)$.
$1326=714\cdot1+612$
$714=612\cdot1+102$
$612=102\cdot6+0$
$\gcd = 102$    $\operatorname{lcm} = \dfrac{714\cdot1326}{102} = 9282$
Part E: Mixed Review / Challenge
18. Two gears have 48 and 60 teeth. If they start aligned, after how many rotations of the smaller gear will they realign?
HintThis is an LCM problem — think about what "realign" means in terms of teeth traveled.
$\operatorname{lcm}(48,60) = 240$ teeth of travel $\Rightarrow 240/48 = 5$ rotations of the smaller gear.
19. A rectangular tile floor is tiled with the largest possible square tiles with no cutting, using a room that is 360 in. by 252 in. What is the side length of each square tile?
HintThink GCD.
$\gcd(360,252) = 36$, so tiles are $36\times36$ in.
20. Explain in your own words why $\gcd(a,b)\cdot\operatorname{lcm}(a,b) = a\cdot b$, using the "shared primes vs. unshared primes" idea from lecture. (Short conceptual answer — self-check against the solution.)
Every prime factor contributes to the product $a\cdot b$ either once (if shared, it's "used" in the gcd) or fully (if not shared, it's "used" in the lcm's extra factors) — splitting the combined prime factors of $a\cdot b$ exactly between gcd and the leftover gives lcm, so their product recovers $a\cdot b$.
